================================================================================
PROJECT PROPOSAL: SENTIMENT-AWARE CONDITIONAL FACTOR MODEL FOR EQUITY RETURNS
================================================================================

PROBLEM STATEMENT
-----------------

Predicting stock returns is one of the most challenging problems in quantitative
finance. Traditional factor models, such as the Fama-French three-factor model,
assume that factor risk premia remain constant over time. However, empirical
evidence suggests that the compensation investors receive for bearing certain
risks varies substantially across different market regimes. During periods of
market stress, for example, investors demand higher compensation for holding
risky assets, while in calm markets, risk premia compress.

This project addresses the fundamental question: How can we build a predictive
model that captures both the cross-sectional characteristics that make stocks
differentially exposed to systematic risks AND the time-varying nature of factor
risk premia driven by market sentiment and volatility regimes?

The key insight is that market sentiment indicators like the CNN Fear & Greed
Index, combined with volatility measures (VIX, EGARCH), credit spreads, and
yield curve dynamics, contain valuable information about the current market
regime. Rather than using sentiment to directly rank stocks (which leads to
overfitting), sentiment should inform which types of risk exposures are
rewarded at any given time.

METHODS
-------

This project implements a Conditional Factor Model architecture with two
distinct components that enforce a clean separation between stock-specific
characteristics and market-wide regime information:

1. STOCK EXPOSURE MODEL - b_i,t = f(x_i,t)

   This component learns latent factor exposures from cross-sectional stock
   characteristics including:
   - Price momentum (1M, 3M, 6M returns)
   - Realized volatility (20d, 60d rolling windows)
   - Rolling beta versus S&P 500 and sector ETFs
   - Liquidity metrics (average daily volume, turnover)
   - Size (log market cap)
   - Fundamental ratios (book-to-market, ROE, debt-to-equity)
   - Sector/industry embeddings

   The model uses either LightGBM or a shallow MLP to ensure robustness and
   low variance. Importantly, this component contains NO sentiment data and
   NO market-wide variables - only stock-specific, slow-moving characteristics.

2. FACTOR PREMIA MODEL - lambda_t = g(m_t)

   This component learns time-varying prices of risk from the market state
   vector, which includes:
   - CNN Fear & Greed Index (with trend/momentum features)
   - VIX level and dynamics
   - EGARCH conditional volatility and standardized shocks
   - S&P 500 distance to 200-day MA, momentum, and drawdown
   - Yield curve spreads (10Y-2Y, 10Y-3M, 30Y-10Y)
   - High-yield credit spreads (HYG vs TLT)
   - Real rates (TIP vs TLT)
   - Market breadth (equal-weight vs cap-weight S&P 500)
   - Cyclical vs defensive sector rotation

   This component uses a Liquid State Space Model (SSM) architecture that
   captures temporal dependencies in market regimes while remaining
   computationally efficient.

The final return prediction is the inner product: r_hat = b_i,t' * lambda_t

Training employs a rolling window walk-forward validation approach with
1-year training windows, 2-week validation windows, and 2-week test periods
that slide forward through time. This prevents look-ahead bias and tests
true out-of-sample predictive performance.

POTENTIAL TECHNOLOGIES
----------------------

The implementation leverages the following technologies:

- Python 3.10+ as the primary programming language
- PyTorch for deep learning model implementation (Liquid SSM, MLP components)
- LightGBM for gradient boosting baseline comparisons
- yfinance API for historical stock prices and fundamentals
- CNN Fear & Greed Index API for sentiment data
- arch library for EGARCH volatility modeling
- pandas/numpy for data manipulation and feature engineering
- Apache Parquet for efficient columnar storage of large datasets (~2000 stocks)
- scikit-learn for preprocessing (StandardScaler) and cross-validation

Hardware requirements include GPU acceleration (CUDA) for training the Liquid
SSM models, though the architecture is designed to be efficient enough for
CPU-based inference in production.

POTENTIAL SOCIETAL AND ETHICAL IMPLICATIONS
-------------------------------------------

1. MARKET FAIRNESS AND ACCESS
   Sophisticated quantitative models like this one could exacerbate inequality
   in financial markets if access is limited to well-resourced institutions.
   The strategies derived from such models may profit at the expense of less
   sophisticated retail investors who lack access to similar technology. To
   mitigate this, the project emphasizes transparent methodology that could
   be replicated by academic researchers.

2. SYSTEMIC RISK CONCERNS
   If similar sentiment-based factor timing strategies become widely adopted,
   they could create correlated trading behavior that amplifies market
   volatility during stress periods. When many algorithms simultaneously
   detect "extreme fear" and rebalance portfolios, the resulting trades could
   worsen the very conditions they are responding to. This feedback loop
   represents a potential source of systemic risk.

3. DATA PRIVACY AND SENTIMENT MANIPULATION
   The CNN Fear & Greed Index aggregates public market data, but future
   sentiment indicators might incorporate social media or alternative data
   sources that raise privacy concerns. Additionally, if market participants
   know that algorithms respond to specific sentiment signals, there may be
   incentives to manipulate those signals.

4. ENVIRONMENTAL CONSIDERATIONS
   Training deep learning models requires computational resources that
   consume energy. This project mitigates environmental impact by using
   efficient architectures (Liquid SSM vs Transformers) and optimized data
   loading pipelines that reduce unnecessary computation.

5. JOB DISPLACEMENT IN FINANCE
   Automated quantitative strategies may reduce demand for traditional
   fundamental analysts and portfolio managers. While this represents
   technological progress, it also displaces workers whose skills may not
   easily transfer to other roles.

6. MODEL INTERPRETABILITY AND ACCOUNTABILITY
   Neural network components of the model are less interpretable than
   traditional linear factor models. When the model makes incorrect
   predictions leading to losses, it may be difficult to explain why.
   This "black box" nature raises accountability questions, particularly
   if the model is used to manage client assets.

CONCLUSION
----------

This project represents an advancement in factor modeling by incorporating
sentiment and volatility regime information into a theoretically grounded
conditional factor framework. By maintaining strict separation between
stock characteristics and market state, the model avoids common overfitting
pitfalls while capturing genuine time-variation in risk premia. The approach
balances predictive power with interpretability by decomposing returns into
economically meaningful components (exposures and premia), making it suitable
for both academic research and practical portfolio management applications.
